• Supreme Court suggests adding unpaid traffic challans to electricity bills 11h ago
Sources: Autocar India ↗
  • The Supreme Court has suggested linking unpaid traffic challans to electricity bills to help recover outstanding fines. The suggestion came during a hearing on electronic enforcement of traffic violations, where the court was informed that states and Union territories issued e-challans of around Rs 45,000 crore, of which about Rs 25,000 crore has been recovered. A bench of Justices JB Pardiwala and KV Viswanathan said issuing e-challans alone was not enough and that authorities needed to focus on recovering the fines. “It is not just sufficient to keep issuing e-challans. Police may issue thousands and lakhs of such e-challans. The question is recovery of fines as sought to be imposed by way of the e-challans,” Justice Pardiwala said, according to Bar and Bench. Court seeks stronger recovery of pending fines The court suggested that unpaid traffic fines could be added to electricity dues, with the possibility of power disconnection providing an additional way to recover the outstanding amount. “If people are not paying traffic challans, add it to their electricity dues bill. They’ll have to pay electricity bills else power line will be disconnected. Work it out. In this country, you have to find a way out,” the bench said. The court clarified that linking challans to electricity bills was a suggestion made during the hearing and not a direction for immediate implementation. Vehicle services could be blocked over unpaid challans The Supreme Court also discussed measures that could restrict vehicle-related services until outstanding challans are cleared. These include stopping renewal of registration certificates, withholding duplicate RCs and blocking ownership transfers. The court also suggested blacklisting vehicles with unpaid challans on the Parivahan portal, withholding fitness and PUC certificates and stopping the renewal of driving licences. It also discussed suspending licences that are already in force. Justice Pardiwala stressed that any measures adopted would need to work in practice, saying, “The authorities need to work at the ground level having regard to ground realities.” Random vehicle checks were also suggested to identify vehicles with unpaid e-challans. Where such a vehicle is found, the court said necessary steps could be taken to impound it. The suggestions were made while the court was hearing a plea concerning electronic enforcement of traffic violations and recovery of unpaid e-challan fines, as part of a long-running 2012 public interest litigation on road safety.
• Suzuki developing lower-cost ADAS that could reach affordable cars 11h ago
Sources: Autocar India ↗
  • Suzuki is planning to make core ADAS functions standard across its future India line-up, including affordable cars, as it develops a lower-cost system suited to Indian road conditions. The aim is to offer greater access to accident prevention. “Fitting expensive advanced driver assistance systems (ADAS) only to a few luxury cars does not reduce accidents across society,” said Katsuhiro Kato, Suzuki’s chief technology officer, at the company’s recent technology strategy briefing, citing more than 1.77 lakh road accident fatalities in India in 2024. Proposed system’s cameras and AI tech to eliminate the need for a front radar Suzuki is also working on lighter bodies designed to protect occupants in crashes Core ADAS functions to become standard Kato said Suzuki intends to make safety functions such as automatic emergency braking and lane departure prevention standard, while reserving additional comfort and convenience features for higher variants. The manufacturer describes the approach as “just-right ADAS”, with the system being developed specifically around Indian traffic conditions and the need to keep costs down. Cameras and AI to eliminate the need for a front radar Image used for representational purposes only. To reduce the cost of the system, Suzuki plans to use high-resolution cameras and AI chips while integrating driver and occupant monitoring with ADAS. The proposed setup will also do away with the front radar sensor. The company says it is targeting “major cost reductions” while maintaining “equal or better safety performance”. These are, however, development targets, and Suzuki has not released performance results for a production version of the system. The manufacturer says it is approaching vehicle safety from two directions: preventing accidents through ADAS and protecting occupants when a crash occurs. Alongside its electronic safety systems, the company is working on lighter vehicle bodies designed to protect occupants in a collision. New electronic architecture could cut costs Suzuki’s ADAS plans form part of its wider “SDV Right” strategy, which aims to simplify the electronic architecture of its future vehicles. The manufacturer plans to combine communication, power distribution and body control functions into a Central Zone ECU. It estimates that this controller could cost around 30 percent less and weigh about 15 percent less than its conventional arrangement. The carmaker has not yet announced which models will get the new ADAS system or when it will be introduced. In the small-car segment, the Baleno is currently Maruti Suzuki’s most affordable model to offer an ADAS suite, which was introduced with its recent facelift. The technology is currently not offered by any of its premium hatchback rivals. With inputs from Kiran Murali
